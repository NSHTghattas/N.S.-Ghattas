
"Wait, what?" Came the confused query from Keundr as he served Luutkesr some coffee later that day.

Luutkesr sipped at the cup, grimaced at it's quality, then set it back down before continuing. "Yeah, there was no murder at all to begin with. It was just dumb, unfortunate circumstance."

"Then what about that gold pit? That makes no sense, everyone keeps saying that Kevsr would have dipped into her reser--"

To ensure that she did not have to listen to that nasally voice drone on for a millennia, Luutkesr interrupted the Pinvixr Brother. "Well, Rucl--my father had a friend look into it and turns out, it's an old coin. *Really old*. And unique."

"And why does that matter?"

"Do you know of anyone who might have had a large surplus of money? One buried somewhere? Specially minted for some odd reason?"

Understanding lit Keundr's features. "No...You're not saying--?"

"Yes I am!" Luutkesr said with delight. "Old Man Kuvengyr's tax hole! Right there next to our village the whole time! Kevsr must have found it recently and began to draw from money the long-dead tax evader had no more use for."

"That is crazy. What about the sign and the tree?"

"The sign had rotted, though the tree is a bit more confusing. Best that we could tell, some animal had been working away at the tree roots for *months*, the work culminating right as Kevsr was under it."

"Well that's just rotten luck."

"Indeed. The question now is, what are we going to do with it?"

"What, you don't get to have any gold?" Keundr asked.

Luutkesr shook her head sorrowfully. "The authorities say it's dirty money that can't be brought into circulation. Some dumb law about unauthorized coin minting. Plus, we use paper currency now."

"Right. You couldn't spend that discretely even if you tried."

"But I didn't get out of this situation with absolutely nothing, either." Luutkesr leaned back onto her chair, her satisfactory. "The court has decided to overlook my crimes."

"That's certainly nice."

Luutkesr sighed as she leaned the chair a bit further back, putting her foot on the table. "But that's not without a few caveats. They say I need to pay a fine. And my father *agreed*."

Keundr nodded along as he glanced around at his and his brother's bustling business. "And you came to me for all this...why? Don't you have a favorite place over that frea--Green man's place?"

"Oh trust me, I'd rather be there. It's just that *Creamy Cups* is filled to the brim with a new rush of people suddenly excited to try their specialty."

"Ah."

"Yeah. Can't say I am happy about that." Luutkesr said as she tried to reach for her cup of coffee, willing to try the concoction again. "But I'm happy *for* him, you know? And I hear Hiptiya might be gaining some interest joining him."

"Good move, asking for her account. Why didn't you do that in the first place?"

Luutkesr got a touch defensive. "Unmasked Green people are a bit...much."

"Can't help but think you could have saved a lot of hardship if you had just swallowed your pride and went to her."

Luutkesr contemplated that for a few moments before replying.

"Hey Keundr?"

"Yes?"

"Shush."

"Right back at you."

"Yeah, right--" Luutkesr overbalanced and fell on her backside, lukewarm coffee spilling all over her.

Keundr looked down at the drenched Luutkesr without any pity, shaking his head before putting the chair back up. He walked away, and as he did so, he called out.

"No refunds."
