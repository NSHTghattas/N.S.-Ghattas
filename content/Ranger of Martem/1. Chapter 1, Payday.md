In the coastal nation of Ninovacia of the Brassen Empire, somewhere in the frontier city of Martem, there lived a woman. She was a foreigner, and her name was Brathis.

She awoke from her bed as she had for every day in the past two weeks. The bed was not made with feathers or hay, for her hard wood-and-metal body required none of the soft comforts of the flesh. It was instead simply a sheet of wood atop an empty bedframe.

She turned her head to take in her new lodgings, having finally just paid for it the Period before. Her head dipped as she contemplated staying in her spot.

However, her job would not work itself.

Getting up from her bed, the woman put on her clothes—a social requirement here despite her lack of anything to hide. First she strapped on a utility belt on her waist, a vital piece of her outfit for her line of work. Then she covered everything else with a cloak the color of sapphire blue. Only her head and lower legs were exposed, all the better to draw less attention.

Ready for the day, she opened the door and exited her lone house on the outskirts of Martem, a good distance from the urban sprawl. Her conical face—making her resemble a minimalistic bird—took in the wilderness that greeted her. Hot sun. Trees the size of buildings. An active wildlife, if somewhat tamed.

Nowhere near comparable to her homeland.

Her footsteps were quiet, instinct taking over as she traversed the landscape towards the main city where she would be assigned her duty for the day.

The well-trod path of dirt and pressed grass of barely explored land eventually turned into the deliberate road of civilization. Buildings began to become more frequent as Brathis noticed people exiting and entering their homes. Farmers with tired eyes went into houses with intent to sleep, different farmers emerged with bleary eyes and a readiness to work.

Many turned their heads warily to watch the intruder amongst them. She was not one in truth, but her perception in the eyes of everyone was the one true law this far from the main capital of Ninovacia.

The people in this part of the world were quite different from Brathis. They had scales like reptiles, their colors ranging the spectrum of the rainbow. Large draconic and reptilian snouts and maws that Brathis' cone-face attempted to mimic. Protruding spines from the backs of heads and necks denoting gender with frills to aestheticize. They were divided into two species that mostly lived in harmony in the Empire: Drakken and Lizardken.

Even their clothes were different, consisting of color hand-made tunics with only one sleeve, embroidered trim running along the cuffs and edges of the garments. At first glance they would appear solid in color, but a closer inspection revealed words traced around the curvature of the tunic sleeves and edges, often speaking of platitudes, family mottos, or simply an excerpt of holy text. Some of them had waist-length capes worn on their side and held by a brooch denoting some significant emblem.

Brathis, on the other hand, was an Ironkin. A machine. Constituent of both processed wood and forged metal made to cooperate by inscribed runes within, she could not be more different from the Ken that lived here. Her few similarities only served to further emphasize her differences, as her 'snout' was the aforementioned cone.

Soon, the employment center was within sight. Compared to the other houses that usually only had a single floor and few rooms, the office had two floors and took the space of half a dozen such houses. Such a size was necessary as this was the central building of operations for most things in Martem.

Opening the door, the interior that Brathis had come to know for weeks was revealed. It smelled of paper, the machine's "nostrils" even detecting what tree the papers came from. Many of the clerks—mostly Lizardken—were hunched over their desks. With pens made of fish scales, they furiously scribbled on the pages, the trails of ink left behind forming the administrative backbone of the city.

The employment center was split organizationally. There was a section for those who needed employment, the clerks assigning basic jobs that required little to not expertise to those unable to find them. The other section was the one that Brathis actually interacted with.

The Martem Rangers.

Shortly after she had arrived at Martem, Brathis had applied to be a Ranger and succeeded. The Martem Rangers were a new initiative to encourage the exploration of the remaining unknown land in the continent. She was tasked every day with a certain job or other that furthered that goal—escorting settlers, scouting for suitable spots for settlements, and protecting the settlers from the feral wilderness, among others.

She passed by a few people waiting in chairs for whatever reason, the "quiet hour" in-between two Cycles when the fewest people were awake being the reason for their scarcity. She stood in a line for a while, even so. People in her line of work tended to get things done early.

It just so happened that getting paid at the end of the week landed squarely within that category.

"Next!" Bellowed out the voice of the lone clerk assigned to the Rangers that day, already aggrieved. The scent of strong tea wafted from a cup, a futile struggle against tiredness.

Brathis took a moment to realize that she was up next. She walked up to the clerk, who was someone she actually recognized. If she had had a fully functional face like the people around here, she would have had a wide smile.

<p>"It's a great day to be paid! Don't you think so, Panmo?" Her cheerful voice was a nearly perfect match for what one would expect a Lizardken woman to sound like, save for the fact that it sounded as if the words were spoken from within a bucket. <span class = "hidden">Of course, she hated that voice, but this was yet one more grievance to be put into the box.</span>  </p>

The man, Panmo, sighed. He rubbed his blue-scaled face with his hand, his other one going for the box in his desk drawer. The Lizardken replied in a not so cheery manner.

"You are not the first one to say that. Nor will you be the last."

He counted out the small, trapezoid shaped coins into his hand, the claw-fingers deftly handling the objects no doubt backed by experience. A few moments was all it took for Ponmo to slide the depressing amount of Ninovacian Marks to Brathis' side. Suffice it to say, this was a lower payout than the woman usually got.

"Why's it so little?" Brathis asked a touch indignantly. Injecting emotions into her voice really was an art with these people. "I know what my contract says, this is much less than the agreed—"

"Don't blame it on me." The clerk cut her off. "Blame the house you burned down."

His reply came in a monotone voice, Brathis too frustrated to actually pretend in having inflections. "I did not burn that house down. It was accidental."

"Accident or not," The clerk replied, "a house still burned down and you were found a culprit, so the fine was deducted from your payment."

Brathis calmed herself. It took a lot of will, but she had been known back home for her pragmatism. She could suffer short term loss.

"Alright then." She nodded, her hand taking the Marks and placing them in her pouch. Switching the subject, Brathis asked Ponmo a question. "Do you not have a job for me today?"

It was always a whole song and dance with these people, the Ironkin thought. They would slight her in some minor way, she would ignore it. She would then ask for a task again and they'd say—

"As a matter of fact, I do."
