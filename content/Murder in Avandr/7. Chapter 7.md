
While she was still smarting from the spectacularly failed interrogation, the amateur Investigator brought in her next suspect. Bicupsr himself.

Now, she was playing a different role here than she had in her previous interrogation. She had played tough because she knew the Fuglind respected few people and needed a firm hand in convincing--no guesses as to how well that went.

Bicupsr was a different story, he needed a deft touch. Too much pressure and he would surely lock himself up in political jargon and empty promises, as slimy politicians tended to do.

So she sat in front of him politely, back held straight. Waiting for him to start the conversation.

Bicupsr, being an old hand at this, was of course experienced with her tactic and deliberately made her wait almost half an hour. Each minute that passed by was keenly felt by the usually impatient woman, but she held through to the end.

Then, the silence was broken after Bicupsr had established his dominance.

Bicupsr spoke. "You know, I do believe I have seen you before. Weren't you the one who stumbled at the Fuglind's door? When I accidentally knocked you over thoughtlessly."

*What was he doing, coming out and mentioning the Fuglind like that?* That was *Luutkesr's* line that would have put him off-balance. Luutkesr took a moment too long to compose a response as Bicupsr continued.

"Of course, the Shack is in a fairly isolated part of the village. It truly is a wonder that you happen to be passing through. And so close that someone may bump into you. Almost like there was something at play here, was there not?"

Oh no. This was bad. This was really bad.

*Bicupsr could actually use his brain.*

"I don't know what you are implying." Luutkesr denied.

"Implying? That would mean that I need to hide my meaning to some degree. Is there, perhaps, something you know that I don't? Maybe you know why you happened to be so close?"

Wait a minute. This was *her* interrogation! This was a harder opponent than the Fuglind.

"I'm the one asking the questions here. I know what you did."

"That was a statement, and no you don't."

"Do, too."

"Do not."

"Do, too."

"Do not."

"Fine, I don't"

"Do too--ah, how clever. Unfortunately it would take more than that to take a confession out of me."

"You just did."

"Oh, that was a rhetorical. You do know what those are, don't you?"

"You think I don't?"

"Well, you seem to--ah, almost caught me again. You are truly a formidable foe. Unfortunately, you will get *nothing* from me."

"Drat."

She was getting nowhere with Bicupsr, and even her best tricks were merely the warmup a politician like Bicupsr used as an opener. She was outmatched. At least, if she played his game.

"How about we play a game? Two truths and a lie, a classic." Luutkesr asked, before continuing. "I get first turn."

"Very well, I'll play along." Bicupsr smiled patronizingly.

"I once ate a whole cake in one sitting. I helped uncover a murder before. I have, on multiple occasions, hit my head on doorframes."

Bicupsr affected a mock gesture of deep contemplation, putting two hands together at the finger tips. "I suppose the second one is the lie?"

Luutkesr grinned triumphantly. "Nope. The first."

"Ah, so you *do* have reasonable eating habits."

Luutkesr felt it wise not to correct him on that. "Your turn."

"Very well. I was born with an extra finger. I self-sabotaged my first date. I did not commit any crimes recently."

"With a spread like that, it must be the third one." Luutkesr said.

"You'd think so, wouldn't you? In truth, it is the second one. Every woman has enjoyed my company."

"Even the Fug--wait, an extra finger?" Luutkesr raised her eye brow.

"Why yes, it's on my left hand. You didn't notice?" He held out said hand, and true to his word, there were six fingers.

"As undeniably interesting as that is," Luutkesr regained her composure, "I assume 'every woman' who has enjoyed your company includes someone like, say, the Fuglind?"

Gone was Bicupsr's cool and measured demeanor as he sputtered in pure shock. "*Me? And her? Together?* Are you out of your mind?"

"So that *wasn't* a romantic back and forth between you two in the Shack? I could swear the tension was *palpable*."

"You're not supposed to listen in on those meetings."

"When has that ever stopped someone like you? I am telling you, I know everything. Tell me. *Why did you murder Kervan?*"

"*I did not*--what?"

"Don't play dumb, there is no use lying."

"You-you think *I* would murder anyone?" Bicupsr exclaimed in indignation. It seemed very real. "Are you insane?"

"Well no, I'm arguing that *you* are."

"You can't convict me, because I well and truly did not do it."

"Oh? Then what else could it possibly be? Your secret conversation with the Fuglind. Your other secret conversation with a mysterious partner at *Eatery*. Your injured leg. Your sudden interest in any politics not related to the poor Venka. All signs lead to hiding a murder."

"*You followed me there?* You must have broken thirteen different laws by now!"

"Not if I was in pursuit of a criminal. Or maybe two? Who were you speaking to, that day?"

"No, you weren't. You went after an innocent man. Also I was alone."

"Alone? Do you just talk to yourself?"

"Don't judge me."

"Fine. But how are you innocent?"

"I am innocent because I was talking about...my favorite birds."

Luutkesr's mind reeled to a stop.

"Your...*favorite* birds?"

Bicupsr sighed, half in the relief of someone revealing a secret, half in resignation that said secret was about to be spread across the whole town.

"I like seagulls more than corvids."

Luutkesr narrowed her eyes. This was a corvids-only town. "I can see why you'd keep that a secret. It would ruin your power. Your reputation. Your entire career. I don't even care about your murder anymore."

"Yes." He sighed.

"So that money you were pushing for? What you were talking to the Fuglind about?"

"I was pushing for funding so that I may eventually build a center to care for the seagulls. The Fuglind preferred geese." Everyone's mortal enemy. "She had found out my plans and threatened to blackmail me if I did not get behind her favored policies."

"That's why you had a dud Investigator? You did not want attention on the murder when money could be better spent for your own ends?"

"Precisely."

"And what about the leg? If you did not injure that trying to stage the seamstress' death, then how did that happen?"

"The Fuglind's goose attacked me."

Pin drop silence.

"This might be the only time you got a politician to be honest, eh?" He tried to joke, pivoting from the bleak topic.

"And I suppose there's a cost to this honesty?"

"Not a word to anyone. Or else you will find your unfounded accusations suddenly coming true."

"Duly noted." Luutkesr said. Now there was the politician she was familiar with.

---
Luutkesr felt she had all the pieces needed to solve this mystery.

At least, that's what she told herself as she brought in the last suspect, already resigned to another failed interrogation. The people bringing the suspect in looked askance, but listened to her anyway.

On the opposite side of her table stood The Chair. It was one of the fancier ones, with a padded seat and everything. The wood, oak, was expertly carved and embellished with designed. The only blemish on this masterwork was an unidentifiable food stain that seemed to resist many attempts to wash it off.

"Do *you* happen to know who murdered Krena, if Bicupsr's innocence is to be believed?"

She got no answer. She sighed in defeat. She really thought she had figured the whole thing out, and so neatly too. Bicupsr had been committing some crime, the seamstress found out and had to be silenced. The Fuglind possibly helped out in exchange for a share in the proverbial riches.

But the Fuglind wouldn't even talk. Bicupsr claimed no murder by confessing to a much greater crime. Nobody would talk to her after the bakery incident. If she did not come up with an interesting answer, the court would convict her. It was all falling apart.

Luutkesr stood up and kicked the Chair. "And all I have to show for it is this stupid oak chair. Not even good pine."

If only oak chairs could talk. Then maybe she could talk to someone who hadn't met her before, then she'd be taken seriously. The only oak-colored things she spoke to were her empty library chairs and--

"...Prohubdis."

*'...know nothing about it sadly. My sister equally little, I would guess, isolated as she is...'*

She may have a lead after all.

"Thank you Chair, you have been a great help."

Where...where did Hiptiya live again?

---
The crowd gathered in the town square. Word was, something interesting was going to happen, and the court (note the lowercase 'C') were here to watch it.

It had something to do with that odd girl, the one who read trashy novels to all fifteen of the people who liked them. She apparently had been investigating something...serious.

On a hastily erected podium, tall enough for everyone to see, stood two figures. One was the tan, pudgy Human people have come to know as that gluttonous bookworm. The other one, however, was familiar in how unfamiliar she was.

The sight of an unmasked Green Woman--a plant-like being--was not something one typically wanted to see, at least this type of Green Woman. It was inhuman, for lack of a better term.

"Ladies and gentlemen of the town!" The annoying librarian announced, trying to draw attention to herself like she was entertaining an audience. "Today I come with news of a murder most foul!"

A murder most foul? Could she mean the already infamous death circulating the the whole town, gold pit and all?

"This whole case, of course, revolves around the murder of one Kerwin. She was bru--"

"*It's Kevsr!*" Someone shouted.

"Huh? I could have sworn--nevermind. Kevsr was found brutally murdered on the road to the farm! She had a bludgeoned head, stubbed toe, and was squished *flat* by a tree."

Some of the audience grew a bit green at this vivid description.

"Now, I have made some...creative assumptions for who might have murdered the poor seamstress. We don't need to get into it, as there were no eye witness accounts for that day, making this almost impossible to solve. *Or so we had thought.*"

A silence fell over the crowd as the implications of what Luutkesr had said fully set in. She gestured to the person next to her. She was a woman made of vines, thorns and flowers. Much like her brother.

"Would you do the honors of describing the unfortunate death of Ms. Kevsr?" Luutkesr asked Hiptiya. She shrugged and stepped forward. She spoke.

It took a few seconds for everyone to realize she was not yelling loud enough to be heard. It took a few more for her to realize that. Giving the impression that she would be blushing had she any--well, blood--she continued at a louder volume.

"The woman, Kevsr, was walking to the farm. She was busy looking at the big ol' pit of gold. She tripped on the Idiot's Stump. Her head impacted the sign, which had slightly rotted. Then a tree fell on her."

A few moments pause.

"That is all."

There was an uproar.
