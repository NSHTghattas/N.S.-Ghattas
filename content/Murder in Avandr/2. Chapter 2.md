
Had this not been a serious matter of death, the scene of the crime might have been a monument to a comedic series of events so improbable that there was almost no possible way for it to have occurred naturally.

From what Luutkesr could conclude from her first glance (eyes conveniently glazing over the dead body), Kevsr the seamstress, a quartz Dwarf, had been on her way to a dug pit with a lot of money stashed in it. The first person to find her had been going to a farm for work, apparently.

Except, she had tripped on something as shown by a scuff mark on a particularly annoying looking rock. Luutkesr recognized it as the one all outsiders managed to trip on. The particularly interesting part, however, began when the woman spotted a dent on a sign, wet from melted snow. It had been put there long ago to warn of said rock, but no one listened to it.

They never bothered to remove the sign, despite even the lettering wearing out.

And then, after what Luutkesr had guessed was Kevsr surviving a head-on collision between her head and the old sign, the rock must have dislodged something since a tree fell on her. A whole tree. It fell on the woman, flattening her like a bug.

The end result of this unfortunate series of events left Kevsr with a stubbed toe and a large bump the size of her fist on her head. Kevsr had seemed to be reaching for the money pit as the tree fell on her, too, judging by her outstretched hand.

Luutkesr finally managed to gather the courage to look at the body, then immediately regretted it. No wonder no one else wanted to be near here, even the most prolific of gossipers wouldn't want to look at..._that_ for the juiciest of tidbits.

It was only because she was half-covered in snow that she didn't completely see the extent of the flattening.

Now that she had a good look at the place, though, something pinged around in Luutkesr's head as if trying to make itself known. Something was off, here. Things were not as it should be.

For one, there were footsteps in the snow around the scene, as if inspecting it. That would be the alleged Investigator that politician had sent, Luutkesr guessed. Judging by the very short circuit, the Investigator had barely given the scene a glance. Definitely not suspicious at all.

Second, why would someone who lived in the village her whole life miss the stone _now_ of all times? It was not like Kevsr had been too high and mighty to tough it out, to hear people talk of her. At least not before she started spending a suspicious amount of money. If _Luutkesr_ had tripped on that rock, she would have disowned herself right then and there if her family did not already do it for her.

And wooden signs didn't bend inwards like metal when hit, they splintered. Unless this wood was different? But why would they use weird wood when making this sign? Maybe it was the wood being wet at this time of year? Either way, she doubted tripping into the sign would cause a bump the size of a fist to form on the head only seconds after impact. She'd know, she banged her head on the short door frames designed for dwarves more times than she cared to count.

Then again, it was quite hard to tell a bump from a flat...

Luutkesr decided to look away from the body.

And finally the tree falling on Kevsr seemed just a little over the top. Trees have sturdy roots, they don't usually just fall down on a whim. And not to mention the _money pit_ itself. Luutkesr turned to look at the hole filled to the brim with golden and silver coins. A bit archaic in terms of money, but it spends just as well as the notes they used around here.

Despite herself, Luutkesr let out a breath of wonder.

"Keundr wasn't kidding. This *is* like Old Man Kuvengyr."

Her eyes narrowed when she saw a hand going just out of view with a handful of the coins in it's grip. She marched to the pit and peeked behind the raised lip of it to see someone hiding in the snow. One she was all too well acquainted with. A man of many careers. All of them failed for one reason or another, of course.

"Don't touch that, Buxingr." She snapped at the crouching figure, causing him to jump in fright.

The man, this one human, looked to Luutkesr before relaxing in relief. "Ah, it's you. Help me out?"

Luutkesr sighed in exasperation. "Brother, please stop. This is just pathetic."

"You don't get it!" He replied. "You think anyone will notice if we skim just a *little* off the top?" Luutkesr raised an eyebrow at the _we_.

"No."

"You get it!"

"I meant; _No, we are not going to do that._"

Buxingr groaned. "You always do this, Luut. Can't you help a brother out?"

The woman did not even dignify that with a response as she hauled her younger brother by the shirt and dragged him away from the crime scene kicking and--well, not quite screaming but whatever it is young men did instead.

She was going to have words in a less open space.

---
Luutkesr marched determinedly up to a house, still dragging her brother behind her. He had stopped complaining long ago, having accepted his fate.

The woman knocked on the door three times, making sure to do it in the distinctive way the occupant of the house explicitly asked for. He did not appreciate unknown visitors. The door opened to reveal an aged man, a Dwarf. He had obsidian skin, though one would not know it from all the saw dust that covered him. Ruclengyr, the Dwarf, must have been sawing a particularly large piece of wood to get all that on him.

He seemed to be scowling when he had opened the door, but it quickly morphed into pleasant surprise when his eyes met Luutkesr's.

"Luut! It's been a long while since you graced my steps." He said, half in welcome and half in reproach.

The woman winced, expecting the almost-lecture but not quite prepared for it. "It has, Ruclengyr."

"And what have I told you about calling me that?" The man said, putting his hands on his hips. "Surely a few months aren't enough to forget your own father?"

Luutkesr glanced at the decidedly non-human form of Ruclengyr.

"I can't say."

The man, her adoptive father, laughed boisterously as he beckoned Luutkesr and Buxingr in. "Come in, come in! I see you've a gift for me as well, if I'm not mistaken. What's he taken this time?"

The brother looked between the two. "I'm _right here_, father."

"Yes, and I'm sure you will fess up yourself." Ruclengyr said.

After the three had had time to settle in the living room of Ruclengyr's home, an austere affair consisting of mainly just a few chairs and a table, Ruclengyr started up the conversation.

He sat down, now cleaned of all the wooden dust, and asked. "So, what have I done for you to come here?"

Luutkesr replied. "Oh, come on Ruc--Father, it's not like that."

"Ah, then what is it you _want me_ to do?"

"It's not--" Luutkesr stopped before she could finish the obvious lie. "Well, not just that. Buxingr's been at it again."

The brother instantly leapt to his own defense. "Didn't steal nothing valuable, I'll have you know."

Luutkesr grew indignant. "You were trying to steal literal gold, what do you _mean_ nothing valuable?"

"It's dirty money, and therefore worth nothing to no one. Father can back me up on this." Buxingr said with infinite wisdom.

"I'm afraid you have that wrong." The dwarf replied as he held out a hand for a coin, cutting further justifications short. Buxingr grudgingly handed him one as Ruclengyr continued. "Dirty money means it was earned illegally. Not that it was literally buried in the dirt."

While Buxingr wrestled with the concept of double meanings, Luutkesr leaned forward in her seat. "So you have heard of the news today?"

Ruclengyr stared at his daughter incredulously. "You think I wouldn't know of it? Everyone and their mother heard the news. I wouldn't have been a good Investigator if I missed _this_ sort of news, now would I?" He referred to his previous career of being an Investigator, a role taken by distinguished people of the law whose jurisdiction spanned the continent. Unfortunately, a bad knee injury and diminishing faculties had led to an honorable discharge.

Luutkesr nodded in acceptance. "Nothing gets past you."

"Dang right."

"Then I'm sure you have an idea of what really happened?"

The elderly former Investigator raised a ruby brow as he thoughtfully inspected the coin. "You don't buy their official explanation?"

"Not one bit."

"That's my girl. I don't rightly believe it either. Kevsr _was_ spending outside of her budget, but she didn't seem the type to stash her money somewhere underground--quite literally in this case."

"How do you know that?" Luutkesr said, curious how her father, master of body language and reading people that he was, came to this conclusion.

Ruclengyr shrugged. "We met often."

Luutkesr's face morphed into surprise. "You knew Kevsr before she died?"

"Of course, we _mature_ folk stick together in a place overrun by children like this."

"Children?"

"Yes, people you're age."

"I'm forty-two, father."

"Exactly my point." He said matter-of-factly. This statement left Luutkesr nonplussed, having forgotten that, yes, Dwarves did indeed live longer than Humans.

"Anyway," Ruclengyr continued. "Kevsr probably did not have some hidden stash of money that she drew from whenever the fancy took her. No, she seemed to be getting that extra payment on top of what she already earned as a seamstress. Regularly, actually, which was at the end of every month."

"Did she, now?" Luutkesr suddenly got _a lot_ more interested. "So the question isn't how she managed to save so much money, but--"

"--But _where_ she was getting it." Ruclengyr completed the sentence.

"Exactly!" Luutkesr exclaimed. Then she looked expectantly at her father. "So did you ask where she got the money?"

"Hah! No, that was her business."

Luutkesr gaped at him, sputtering. "H-how? You're an _Investigator_! Aren't you supposed to ask about those things?"

Ruclengyr mimed zipping his mouth shut. "I don't ask her where she gets her funds, she doesn't ask what I did back in '23."

Luutkesr raised a brow. "What *did* you do back in '23?"

The man crossed his arms. "Nothing as far as the Entertainer's Guild is concerned. The point is, Kevsr needed to draw more money recently. So much to the point I'm surprised whatever was supplying her hasn't run out or been cut off by this point."

"How much could she _possibly_ spend in these visits?"

The elderly man said, a touch defensively. "Competitive Dominoes is a very serious business."

The woman facepalmed, before continuing. "Anything interesting on that coin?" She had noticed her adoptive father's increasing confusion when looking at it.

"You could say that...I will need to look further into this later."

"Regardless," Luutkesr said, "I believe whomever was paying her _did_ cut her off. It's just that it was violent."

Ruclengyr snapped his fingers. "There we go!"

Buxingr, who had seemed to be deep in thought, snapped up. He looked at the two conversing people for a moment before making a triumphant sound.

"Aha! That money's actually classified as _blood money_, which means that I can--"

"_No._" Luutkesr and Ruclengyr said in unison. Luutkesr then turned back to Ruclengyr.

"I investigated the crime scene."

Ruclengyr quickly spoke his dismay. "First, we don't yet know it's a crime. Second, don't go near those. Don't want to go mucking up all the evidence for when the investigator comes in."

"But one already did come in, according to the politician."

"Impossible, it takes at least a day for one to actually look properly at the scene."

"Well, it's what he said at any rate."

"That is not good news. One of the only reasons they would botch an investigation like this would be that they did not want to pay the cost of a certified Investigator."

"Or...?" Luutkesr prompted.

"Or that they were hiding something."

"Interesting." Luutkesr said, tapping her finger to her chin.

"What about the crime scene itself, did you find anything interesting?"

Luutkesr internally smirked at his use of _crime scene_, but decided to let it lie. "Well, tell me how this sounds. Would you ever trip on that rock, the Idiot's Stump, on the path to the farm?"

She got an instant reply. "Never."

"And how plausible does it sound for the old sign to bend when someone hits their head real hard on it?"

"Not entirely plausible, no wood simply bends unless you got some of that weird Steelic wood. Or maybe the sign's rotted."

"And finally, what can move a tree to fall on a struggling Kevsr who was prone?"

"Not much, we'd hear the efforts for miles."

"Then," Luutkesr concluded. "I am fairly confident that there's been a murder."
