
*Golden Street* was crowded with more people than usual, Luutkesr noticed as she petted her friend's parrot. And no wonder, too. Bicupsr was making quite a spectacle there.

She recognized him as the same limping man from next to the *Pinvixr Brother's* coffeehouse, short cropped beard, ugly nose and all. His leg was bandaged too, as if she needed any more proof. He stood atop a pedestal, speaking loudly and projecting his voice quite powerfully to the listening audience.

"Political Speech Here." With a provocative statement like that, he had the crowd's attention wrapped around his thumb, and he knew it too. "Something Dumb."

Luutkesr...did not hear a single word he said. She tried. She really did. But any speech that came from the politician was greasy as he was; it slid right off her. The main thing she got was that he was suddenly switching his stance on something or other, and even then that was only due to the tipoff from that receptionist.

Luutkesr resolved to actually find out about Bicupsr's political messages after going cross-eyed trying to follow the political yammering. The crowds seemed enthused, though.

After another hour of Bicupsr spewing nonsense, he thanked the crowd in a mock-humble manner for their time and attention before beginning to leave. This was Luutkesr's queue to follow him. *Discretely.*

At least, as discrete as a woman bearing a parrot on her shoulder could be.

Blending in with the crowd as to be inconspicuous, Luutkesr observed Bicupsr like a hawk watching it's prey as he limped away. Only a day or two since Luutkesr had found him that way, so it wasn't any wonder he was still limping.

According to that receptionist, word on the street was Bicupsr had bravely suffered a vicious bird attack protecting a baby. Luutkesr somehow doubted that.

The amateur Investigator followed after the man at a remove, largely sticking to crowds for it's invaluable cloak of anonymity. The drawback, of course, was that she ran into a number of people who would have loved nothing more than to take up her time with pleasant conversations.

"Hey, Luut! How's the book--"

"--so long since I last saw you, won't you--?"

"--aware that Avandr is at hand, but that doesn't mean taxes should wait--"

"--old Ruclengyr doing--?"

Luutkesr had a simple strategy of avoiding the busybodies. She simply ignored them as she walked past, not even stopping for a moment. Was it rude? Of course it was, and she would surely pay dearly for the indignity. But it was efficient, and she could not afford to lose Bicupsr while she still had him in her sights. If only the parrot wasn't repeating the annoying words in her ear.

"*You should come visit sometime!*"

"Quiet, you."

Bicupsr himself walked a circuitous route that went down more and more obscure streets that had fewer and fewer people. Luutkesr began to have trouble maintaining her precious anonymity. It certainly did not help that he would glance back occasionally--almost nervously--like he could be caught doing something he wasn't supposed to.

Luutkesr had him, she could practically feel it.

But something was odd. Despite the emptier streets, the less well-made houses and the general atmosphere of 'keep out', Luutkesr knew full well where this route led. This was not the path one took to visit somewhere that had *certain people* who fooled around with *certain substances* (Luutkesr was partial to pastries, anything stronger was too much). Nor did it lead to the rather bleak cemetery where a certain diabolical mad man might laugh and gloat over the headstone of the late Kevan. Poor Kevla.

No, this route could lead to only one place; the Fuglind's shack. What business did *he* have with that old woman? Was she a fellow conspirator in his schemes? Was she involved in Kevana's death?

The onion of crime is peeled away, revealing many layers and machinations unfolding. The knife of truth must cut through all the same, heedless of the tears.

Luutkesr regretted missing breakfast.

---
Luutkesr crept behind the closed shack door, ears pressed against the wood so that she may hear better. While she could hear inside, it was just barely, as the thick walls and the low murmurings of the voices did their job and obfuscated this obviously private conversation. She heard Bicupsr settle himself in the very creaky chair, presumably facing the Fuglind. Now came the good part, what could this man possibly want with the Speaker?

A few passersby gave Luutkesr's crouched form odd looks, as if *she* were unusual for so obviously eavesdropping on an allegedly private meeting between a child and a tired Fuglind. She just ignored them. It was amazing just how much you could accomplish if you simply had no shame.

She managed to catch a few faint sentences through the door, particularly when the voices got heated. She was not able to distinguish between the voices, only vague words.

"...needs the money..."

"...most selfish...!"

"...be reasonable..."

And then the best one yet, so loud and firm that it was obviously the Fuglind's voice.

"*Do it, or I will tell them everything.*"

Oh this was *good*. Either Bicupsr and the Fuglind were having a torrid affair--even Luutkesr slightly hesitated at that conclusion--or she had some incriminating blackmail on Bicupsr. Blackmail like, say... his involvement in the Kaneva murder? Embezzlement? Kicking precious little hatchlings?

And it couldn't have been a bluff, if she read the room right. Bicupsr was groveling before a woman many years his senior who served as a glorified suggestion box.

Then Bicupsr apparently managed to dig up his rarely-used backbone as he delivered a rebuttal.

"I will not fold before some *crone* to forsake my ideals! *I will not bend the knee before everything I stand against!* Good day, Ma'am!"

Stellar delivery, no wonder he was a politician. Luutkesr waited eagerly to listen to what happened next. Maybe the Fuglind had something just as scathing to say? Maybe she would admit to being wrong? A dramatic exit would not be amiss either.

Wait. Wasn't Luutkesr at the door--?

Luutkesr was knocked back on her backside by Bicupsr's accurately predicted dramatic exit, the door having shoved her. She managed to not fall flat on her back, looking up to meet Bicupsr's gaze as he looked down at hers as the parrot was dislodged off her shoulder.

*His eyes. While they were brown, those eyes were far from dull or mundane. His hair was groomed to perfection in a way befitting his station. His cheekbones were high enough to lend him an air of dignity, but not so high as to rob him of his youth. His nose was so...*

*So...*

So, Luutkesr couldn't really get into it as she did not instantly fall in love with the suspect via Clumsy Yet Romantic Collision. You could not blame someone as bookish as she for trying.

"Who are you?" Bicupsr asked.

"Mail for Ms. Fuglind, sir." Luutkesr instantly replied.

"Ah...she's in there."

The parrot hovered back down to land on Luutkesr's shoulder. "*Quiet, you.*"

"...good day." He nodded to the shack, then stepped around Luutkesr and began to limp his way somewhere else.

Of course, Luutkesr had to go into the shack, or else her already thin lie would fall apart. But she also did not mind asking for the juiciest bits of what she couldn't hear from the Fuglind herself.

"Hello, me again! I was wondering if--"

"*Get out!*"
