
It was snowing when the farmer woke up in the season of Avandr, not particularly feeling the holiday spirit. The Sun was already up high in the sky. Then again, the Sun rarely moved.

The farmer shook the grogginess from her head before dressing herself for work, anticipating another cold day. Only a few weeks into the winter, and already she wished the blistering heat of the sun would vent onto the poor, cold populace. Veksr grumbled as she raised a hand to feel the falling snow. Cold. The dwarf looked for her hat. Finding the straw hat, she dusted it off and put it on.

Now she was ready for the day.

She grabbed her tools and made her way from her home on the outskirts of town down to the farm she was contracted to work on. She did not particularly care for her current employer--he was a sad old fart with nothing better in his life worth doing than annoying others--but the pay was good.

She chased off a few wild ravens that had taken an interest in what she was doing as she travelled. She navigated a particularly tricky frozen pond. She stepped carefully over a jutting rock--called the Idiot's Stump--on the path to the farm land, avoiding tripping like many foreigners.  She sidestepped the body next to the pit full of gold. She walked over the fallen tree--

_Wait, what?_

---

"_-and the house, too._" A voice spoke, reading aloud. The speaker was a woman. Her skin was somewhere between really tanned and dark-skinned, and her build was less lean than most working laborers in the village were. A polite way of saying she liked to indulge in more food than was strictly necessary. She read with a clear, smooth voice from a book at a lectern to an audience of listeners. Her name was Luutkesr.

She was currently reading out a book, _Friendly Troubles_, in the library of the village. That was her job. Well, most of it anyhow. The library made a modest sum of money, enough so that they could occasionally splurge on a nice book to read. Most people, while literate, still appreciated listening to stories as opposed to reading them.

Hence, Luutkesr being the appointed book reader.

"_Why? Why would you do this to me, friend?_" Luutkesr continued in a high, falsetto voice. "Xengyr turned back to the person she used to call friend. _Even now, you think we are friends still? Face it. We are done._"

A few of the audience exclaimed their dismay for the deepest bonds of friendship--no, _sisterhood_--being broken, calling out as if to convince the fictional characters to change their minds. As if they would listen.

Luutkesr gave those outspoken people a look, silencing them. After a moment, she cleared her throat and resumed reading. "_Fine,_ She said. _Be that way._ She stormed off, slamming the door to both the room, and their reconciliation." She closed the book with the conclusion of the chapter.

The audience, a staggering grand total of five, clapped for the reading. Luutkesr bowed, engaging in exaggerated theatrics. "Thank you, thank you." She said as everyone stood from their seats and began to leave.

As the last person left the library, Luutkesr found a chair and slumped in it, a contrast to the image of her of just a few moments ago. Not many people had showed up for the reading this time, which she found odd. Usually at least twice their number came. Then again, maybe they found something more interesting than hearing her read the same book for the fifth time this year. No new book in sight.

Sighing in disappointment, Luutkesr decided that an empty stomach was a sad Luutkesr. She sat forward on her seat as she contemplated her meal options. Her sister, Pemdr, was sure to have prepared breakfast by now. No doubt she had cheaped out on the ingredients--_again_--but at least it would be moderately good for her health. She _had_ been putting on more weight that she would have liked. But then again, the bakery was just across the street from the library. The one with the good pastries...

Luutkesr shook herself from the pastry induced haze. She would settle for Pemdr's cooking today. She had to stay strong. She had to watch her waistline. She needed to prove she had an iron will.

She could do this.

---
Luutkesr exited the bakery with a pastry already held in her mouth as she carried a bag full of them. She could eat healthy tomorrow. This said nothing bad about her self control, she was sure. She put it out of her mind as she began to walk home.

The woman was about to reach into the bag, contemplating an attempt to stuff a second baked good into her mouth with the first one still in there, when she noticed something on the street. There seemed to be a moderately large gathering of people talking in one of the coffee houses. Many of whom she recognized. Luutkesr's eyes narrowed.

_So this was where most of the book crowd went._

Abruptly changing course, she made her way straight to the open establishment, not bothering to read the large sign on top of the building, since she already knew the name of this particular coffee house. _The Pinvixr Brothers_.

The patrons were so engrossed in whatever they were talking about that they did not notice Luutkesr dragging a vacant chair over to their seating and staring at them. She leaned forward in a vaguely patient pose--like she were a mother who caught her children doing something they shouldn't--and tried to meet any one of their eyes. She got tired of holding the pose after a couple of minutes, so she just sat back and waited for someone to notice her. Unfortunately for her, the first to notice was someone she did not particularly relish talking to.

"Luutkesr, is that you?" A masculine voice asked from behind, with that nasal quality Luutkesr had come to associate with a headache. The wannabe-sleuth, amateur reader, and professional Magnet For Annoying Barristers turned to look at one of the two male dwarves working the coffee house. Keundr, son of Pinvixr, smiled at her.

Luutkesr nodded, trying to remain as polite as possible as she replied. "It is! Just came by to see what was happening over here."

The dwarf, whose skin looked to be made of stone, raised a gem-like brow. "You are saying you _haven't_ heard the news?"

"What news?" Luutkesr had not been aware that anything happened the day before. Was the council cutting the book buying budget again?

"Oh dear, you really do not know." Keundr said in that irritating voice of his. "Kevsr was found dead!"

"Oh no!" Luutkesr gasped in shock. After a moment, she hesitantly asked. "Who was Kevsr?"

Keundr shrugged. "Don't know. Some seamstress. A farmer found her flattened next to a pit filled with money."

"Like Old Man Kuvengyr?" She was referring to a legendary Tax Evader (so iconic to the degree that his title as Tax Evader is capitalized) who famously hid his misbegotten wealth in a secret Tax Hole buried within a pit. He died before he could actually use the money, it's location lost to time.

Not like he could have ever used it. Crazy man minted his own illegitimate coins.

"Apparently so."

This revelation set wheels turning in Luutkesr's brain. This did not make sense. A pit of money? Her first thoughts were; _'Why was there money in a pit?'_, followed by _'Was it Kevsr's?'_ She had to know what had happened, and why. 

Luutkesr thought of herself as a curious person and a sleuth in these things. Someone who could not let a good mystery lie, who had to discover the **truth**. Her siblings called her 'a nosy bookworm with nothing better in her time to do'.

"Where did the farmer find--" But Keundr interrupted her by pointing at something.

"Look, it's one of the big name politicians!" He said. "Let's pester him."

The gossip circle apparently had the same idea, since they stood from their seats as one and rushed at the politician, engulfing him in a sea of busybodies.

"Sir! Sir! Any word on Kevsr's death?" One of them asked.

"Why was there money in a pit?" Another said.

"Was Kevsr in a conspiracy?"

"Settle down, folks. Settle down." The politician said with placating hands. He was a tall man, a Human like Luutkesr, middle-aged with a short cropped beard. The model of an attractive politician if not for his unfortunate broken nose. "I cannot speak on this right now, we are still investigating it."

"Well what _can_ you tell us?" One of them asked.

The politician seemed to hesitate for a moment before answering. "I'm not supposed to talk about it, but the investigator suspects it's a dispute over wealth."

*An Investigator, already?* That had been quick.

This sent the group murmuring amongst themselves. A few _had_ noticed that Kevsr had been spending more money than she ought to have, certainly nothing a seamstress' wages would allow.

"She must have been blackmailing someone with money!" One of them jumped to a suitably juicy conclusion.

"No, that's not right. She's been hoarding money from some other job. It has to be that!"

"What job could possibly make that much?"

"...I hear rat catching is a very lucrative business."

"You're an idiot."

"Now, now," The politician interrupted. "No need to fight over the right explanation. One will be given that you are all surely going to be satisfied with."

"Is any of us right?"

The man just smiled, then excused himself away from the crowd, slightly limping. Poor man, someone must have stepped on his foot.

"That confirms it," The speaker said. "He's saying my proposed logical conclusion was right."

"You wouldn't know a logical conclusion if it proposed to you."

As the gossipers devolved into heated arguments, Luutkesr decided she needed to get out before she was swept in. She asked Keundr where the scene of the murder was, then said her goodbyes. She had some things to mull over. She reached into her bag to withdraw another pastry as she made her way to the scene of the crime. For it was surely a crime.

Her hands did not find another pastry. She must have eaten them all.

Tomorrow, she'd lay off the things.
