
While Ruclengyr went and found someone to help him decipher the old coin, Luutkesr pondered.

Logically, what should a sleuth do next? Most would argue chasing down the leads relentlessly in the search for the bare truth. Some might suggest taking a moment to plan things through, ever prudent.

Fewer still might call for help, thinking this task too big for them.

Luutkesr elected to take a break. She went back to the bakery, tried to hide her identity, failed, then guilty-ate her new batch of pastries while walking in the snow.

Pinnacle of professionalism, that one.

But beneath that pudgy, ill-willed exterior, a mind was at work. Machinations that'd elude most of the world ran through that head at the speed of thought. Ideas were formed then discarded. Concepts played with, providing inspiration. Her mind was a whirlwind of thought, a well of innovation, a massive--

Luutkesr tripped on something hidden in the snow and dropped everything. She groaned in frustration as she stood up and dusted herself off, grumbling about wasted money.

She needed someone to bounce off of, if this case was going to be solved. Her father had already made his feelings clear on the matter despite his help, her brother was useless, and her sister doubly so.

Her book friends, the people who came to her readings, were rather dull when it came to anything not related to books. She didn't even need to articulate why she'd not go to the *Pinvixr Brothers* coffee house.

But then she got an idea. At this time of the year, who was someone always available for conversation and requests?

---

"--Now, if someone *had* fallen on a sign post with their head, don't you think there'd at least be a *little* bit of blood splatter? Or splintered wood stuck in the forehead? Someone I know says that the sign could be made of something from that Steelic continent, but I'm not convinced." Luutkesr spewed forth a torrential downpour of words upon the poor soul she had cornered.

For the person sat in front of her, on the outskirts of the village in the Avandr Shack, was none other than the *Fuglind* of the village, Goldic for "Bird Speaker". After all, it did not hurt if they had someone who hinted to the Great Bird itself what this humble little community might want for Avandr, did it?

Wonderful Avandr, the season of gift-giving and debt-forgiveness. Just like Big Bird Fuglef would have done.

Of course, when children (and occasionally some adults) usually visited the Fuglind, it was to make a request of some kind of the Fuglef. But nothing prohibited anyone from just sitting in that seat and pestering the poor Speaker in the pretense of a request.

The bereaved Fuglind, an old Dwarven woman with cracked skin (a bit disturbing to people not familiar with Dwarves, but practically the same as wrinkled skin), tried to cut in a few times as she had been trapped for almost an hour at that point. "And you want--"

Only to be interrupted by Luutkesr. "But then again, I'm sure that something *had* to have hit that sign for some reason. Maybe an attempt at concealing the murder? Because that's what I'm sure that is, murder."

"As interesting as that is, what is it yo--"

"And don't even get me *started* on how badly placed those bruises were. I am *intimately* familiar with head trauma, you see."

"*I'm sure you are.*" Muttered the old lady.

"For example, bruises don't form quick enough for it to be the size of a fist if you get flattened by a tree. Also, they were slightly to the right side of her head when it looked for all the world that she fell on her left side. So unless she had turned her head to a worrying angle, she's been clubbed on the head. A clear method of murder. But what's the motive?"

"You came here to get me to ask Fuglef to deliver you a motive? Because neither I nor the Fuglef are in the habit of solving crimes." The Fuglind said flatly.

"That would help, but it's not why I'm here. Just wanted someone to talk to."

"And you came to *me*?"

"What, can't you give me this one gift?"

"Get out before I sic my goose on you."

---
It was snowing when Luutkesr was unjustifiably kicked out of the Fuglind's Shack for 'wasting her time'.

This time the First Bell struck again, but it's cadence was different. Time for sleep, it would seem.

As Luutkesr navigated her way home, she saw an array of different faces than she was used to coming out of their homes, eyelids heavy from grogginess. Second Cycle people were waking up, at least the early birds were.

Luutkesr herself entered her home, which she shared with Pemdr. Turning the key, she ducked into the house (dang Dwarven-height door frame) and announced herself.

"I'm home!"

The first response she got was loud snoring. Typical, of course. The second was a *squawk* noise rapidly approaching. Luutkesr bent down to pet the large crow that flew up to her with a smile on her face.

Luutkesr had her dinner, then dropped on her bed and slept soundly.

Or at least she tried to.

Her mind was still chipping away at the mystery, refusing to let it go. She replayed conversations over and over, as if there was a vital detail she had missed. Seamstress gets new allowance. Seamstress spends said allowance for a while. Seamstress is killed in an 'accident'. Suspected foul play.

She had discussed the oddness of the death to...well, death. No one simply kicked the Rock hard enough dislodge a tree, especially on accident. And the head wound was most definitely malicious. And then the flattening was just mean. Who would go to such lengths to cut someone off from a steady allowance of money?

It would have to be someone rich, and highly placed. Rich, obviously, for the money. Highly placed would give motive for keeping this a secret due to pride or shame. Maybe it was blackmail? Or sending a message?

But then again, a death that convoluted may still be possible. Too bad that limping politician couldn't--

Wait.

The politician had been limping, hadn't he? From what, she wondered.

What if, she supposed, the politician had done the dirty deed? Obviously the idea is silly. Busy and bureaucratic men and women like him didn't bother themselves with anything more strenuous than lifting stacks of paper or signing their names. Their political, underfed bodies simply could not handle the strain.

But would that not make the perfect murderer? Who were people more likely to believe was the killer. Some random politician, or a more worldly farmer with a grudge?

And going back to that limp, that could be connected to the crime. But why would he kick the rock on purpose when it was Keva..Kevn..that woman who was supposed to have kicked it? Luutkesr snapped her fingers in comprehension.

"Because it wasn't on purpose."

Farmers knew that rock, laborers knew that rock. Even *Luutkesr* knew that rock. But what of the people who spent all day in stuffy offices, concerned only with public opinion? That politician--whose name Luutkesr now really wishes she could remember--must have tripped *hard* while dragging the body.

Motive. He may have been blackmailed into sending that seamstress money, before deciding her death was the only way to escape.

Method. Something to club the head. Maybe took a rock and smashed at one of her feet. Still unclear how a spindly man managed to fell a tree.

But Luutkesr did not need to know all of it. There was one suspect, and boy was he suspicious!

That slimy, greasy no-good politician was surely the cause of death for poor what's-her-name.

She finally drifted off to sleep with one last thought.

*She'd get him tomorrow.* Of that, she was sure.
