
Luutkesr was back on the streets, stalking (in a not-so-creepy manner) Bicupsr. He did not seem to be done with his errands yet, walking somewhere else with a purpose.

This time Bicupsr took a more respectable route, making his way to the one upscale eatery that this village had, *The Golden Fork*.

This village had a theme in it's names that one might notice.

Fortunately, this was also one building Luutkesr could follow Bicupsr in without attracting his attention. Unfortunately, this visit would not be good for her wallet.

It seemed the waiter thought so too, as when she came past the door to be greeted by him, the first thing he did was look critically at her rather unassuming attire and her unexpected friend.

"Are you lost?"

"I ask myself that question every day." Luutkesr fired off the reply before she could formulate a more coherent response.

"*It's true.*" The parrot squawked.

"Ah...right this way, miss." He seemed sympathetic, though his professional demeanor gave nothing away. "Do not bring in the bird, though. I will put him with the others."

After someone had come to take care of the parrot while she entered, Luutkesr was seated at an isolated table. To hide her lack of luxury no doubt. Luckily, her table happened to be next to Bicupsr's table. The drawback was that they were separated by a wooden panel, as every table here was in an alcove-like opening. Good for private meetings between associates. Less than ideal for eavesdropping.

She, of course, did not let this deter her. It seemed that Bicupsr was actually talking to someone, though she could not hear the other person. Not surprising, Bicupsr's carrying voice was already faint to begin with.

"...then I was threatened. By a woman more than twice my age! Me! As if I were not the one holding all the cards..."

"..." The other participant must have replied with something thoughtful, as Bicupsr soon sighed before continuing.

"...attack was real...but...money needed...maybe--"

"Your order, miss?" A voice interrupted the dropping of eaves. That waiter was back, ready to take Luutkesr's order. No doubt prepared to make her poorer than she would have liked. She, however, had a trick up her sleeve.

"Water, please."

"You came to *The Golden Fork*," The waiter asked, incredulous. "For *water*?"

"For research purposes." Luutkesr was compelled to provide a reason. She was also right, in a sense.

"I see." He looked at her appraisingly. "What kind of water do you want?"

*What?*

"One of everything you have." Luutkesr said offhandedly, wanting to be left alone.

He hesitated at this, then nodded resolutely before leaving.

Luutkesr sighed in relief, then went back to listening in on...silence?

She risked a peek around the panel to see what had happened. Where Bicupsr had sat, there was now only a bill. And of his other conversationalist, there was nothing to be found but an empty oak chair. A fancy one, though.

A few minutes later, the waiter came back with five different glasses of water and a check.

"That will be two hundred marks."

"For *water*?"

She should have ordered food instead.

---
Luutkesr now had three suspects, but she was not any closer to solving the murder. It seemed that every possible answer only served to unveil more questions. Why were the Fuglind and Bicupsr involved in something? Did it have anything to do with Kervans? How did Bicupsr manage to uproot that tree? Where could Luutkesr find a bakery that had not yet blacklisted her?

Focus. Her suspects were as follows.

Bicupsr, politician of the village, advocate of... something. Had the most incentive to see that seamstress killed, if she shared in his blackmail/embezzlement.

Fuglind, real name unknown, local Speaker to the Big Bird. She might have been one more benefactor of this whole mess. She certainly had sway in the elderly community, if Luutkesr was not far off in her estimated of the Old People Hierarchy.

Chair, property of *The Golden Fork*, presumably Bicupsr's associate. Probably the other side of the conversation (as a malicious listener) and co-conspirator. The perfect criminal, as no one would suspect the chair.

She paced around in her office, which happened to be her bedroom as her shared house with Pemdr was far too small. Lines of yarn trailed around the whole room in a tangled line of thoughts made physical, linking to scribbled notes in chalk wherever Luutkesr could comfortably reach.

This was getting difficult, putting together the crime ingredients of this murder cake. But at least she had some time--

Why did she feel faint?

---

Luutkesr awoke, her mind instantly racing to discover where she was. Her eyes opened with heaviness in her eyelids. She looked down at herself, seeing red smeared on her hands. Heart beating thunderously.

*Was...was this her blood?*

Then she realized she was being prodded awake.

"Wake up! Get up, you glutton! *What have you done?*"

"Wuh?"

"*My bakery! What have you done to my bakery?"

Luutkesr looked around her, sitting up. There were crumbs all over her, forming a trail to the nearest shelf containing baked goods. At least, what had once contained baked goods.

The fact that she had not had blood on her hands, while coming as quite a relief, was immediately pushed away in her mind as she scrambled to make an excuse. (Licking her fingers revealed it was strawberry jam. Delicious.)

Come on, she was Luutkesr. Luutkesr of the Myriad of Excuses. Luutkesr of the Various Justifications. Luutkesr the Smooth Talker! There was absolutely no way that she would fumble coming up with a plausible explanation.

"I...don't know." She lamely said.

She fumbled coming up with a plausible explanation.

---
Fourth Bell sounded throughout the village, just two hours before the change of the Cycles that would herald Luutkesr's time to sleep. Or it would, Luutkesr thought as the tones of the huge village bell resonated through her bones.

She stood before a court. Not an official Court, that had the 'C' capitalized. This one was a collection of self-appointed 'civil judges' that was definitely *not* sanctioned by the official Council (again, note the capitalization).

Despite the flagrant disregard of their ultimate authority, the Court still politely ignored it. It was less paperwork in the end.

All this information flashed through the amateur Investigator's head as she looked at the people who would be judging her for her conduct the past few days. The only thing more surprising than how she came to be here was the identity of one of the four judges before her.

Why was Ruclengyr among them?

"Luutkesr." One of the judges, an old Human woman, announced to the court. "You stand before us today because we have received numerous complaints about your conduct."

Like a well choreographed play, another judge picked up from there. A middle aged male Dwarf. "With charges like 'Breaking and Entering', 'Public Disturbance', and 'Refusal of Greeting.'"

"Refusal of Greeting?" Luutkesr asked, confused.

"Not saying 'Hello' back. Now quiet." Ruclengyr replied for the first time, speaking as if he had never known his adoptive daughter.

*She was brought in for that?*

"You have also been reported throwing baseless accusations at our beloved Bicupsr." The last judge, a young man, continued. "What do you have to say to yourself, Luutkesr?"

"I had good cause!" Luutkesr called out. "I suspect that Bicupsr is a *murderer*!"

An affronted gasp arose from all but Ruclengyr. Then the older woman said. "Do you have proof?"

"Not..much, but I have reason to believe it! Bicupsr killed that seamstress!"

Ruclengyr's brows furrowed as the other three murmured. Then the young one spoke again. "Why should we listen to anything you have to say? You're here for breaking into the bakery and eating half it's inventory."

"I must not have eaten for a bit." Luutkesr's face twisted into a grimace as comprehension dawned on her. "When I haven't eaten well, sometimes I just black out and wake up surrounded in crumbs. Tasty, tasty crumbs."

"By this, I assume you are implying that this 'murder case' is the reason you have been so overworked?" The old woman said flatly.

"Yes." Luutkesr said.

The four judges conferred quietly as they let Luutkesr nervously wait. Sweat ran in rivers down her back.

After a few minutes, they broke apart and the middle aged judge began. "By all rights, the law would say you ought to be thrown to the jail for a day or so. And that your sentence be extended for slander against a politician."

Luutkesr sighed in resignation. "Of course, I unders--"

"I wasn't finished." He interrupted her. "Luckily for you, we are *not* the official authority. And, frankly, we want to see where this goes."

Luutkesr's head snapped up. "What?"

"We weighed the gravity of your crimes against the drama of what you promised to reveal." The woman had the conversation again.

"And we are *seriously* bored." Ruclengyr said with a glint in his eye.

"B-but you are--were an *Investigator*! Aren't you on the side of the law?" Luutkesr was still flabbergasted.

"Not anymore."

"Well...I will see what I can do." The nascent Investigator promised.

"That is all we ask." The young man said. "You can either follow through on this, or we can get our jail cell ready. I hear it's cold this time of year."

"I think I would prefer to investigate." Luutkesr said hurriely.

"Excellent!" The old woman said. "Stamp this contract with your signature."

Without a word, Luutkesr took the stamp, raised her hand--

---
--and slammed it down menacingly on the table, rattling it. Had there been any objects on it, they would have been knocked off. All the more unfortunate, she wanted the intimidation that came with it.

It would not have mattered though, as the suspect seated before her did not react to the loud noise. The suspect appeared thoroughly unimpressed.

Luutkesr was not fazed. She may not have won the first of many small, infinitesimal psychological battles, but this was a war of attrition that favored her. She would grind down her resistance. Stamp out her defiance. Show no mercy.

She would get to the bottom of this, no matter what it took--

"Are you just going to stare at me?" Her suspect interrupted Luutkesr.

She had the good grace to blush a little bit.

"Ahem. Yes." She began, then transitioned into a more serious tone. "*Now.* Tell me about Bicupsr."

The suspect looked flatly at Luutkesr. She was the Fuglind, as it so happened, and she could give a *look* when she really wanted to.

No response was forthcoming.

"Don't make this any harder than it has to be." Luutkesr prompted, injecting some steel into her voice.

The Fuglind remained resolute in her silence, harder to crack than the toughest of nuts. She finally raised a brow in amusement, showing the first true emotion in the whole interrogation.

Luutkesr sighed, then admitted defeat in the face of a much stronger opponent. She was *good.*

"...please?"

The Fuglind found it in her heart to relent after such a heartfelt plea. "You know I can't disclose what goes on in the Shack."

She was right, in a way. The Fuglef's generosity was famously paired with discretion. Every good act was done at the quietest of times, where no eyes were looking. The Fuglef was as secretive as it was benevolent. By association, all the Fuglinds followed the same precept of secrecy and confidentiality.

Luckily, Luutkesr was well-versed on Fuglef's 'laws' due to Buxingr's brief stint as a Fuglind, which had not quite panned out (as his litany of past careers could attest to).  She was prepared. "Not according to our law."

The Fuglind replied without hesitation, performing the song and dance she no doubt encountered several times.

"The Bird's law is my law."

"You forget that the Bird never actually made any laws."

"It's a law by implication."

"That's not how it works, and you know it."

"Who's the Bird Speaker here, you or me?"

It was getting clear that Luutkesr would not be able to get any more information on that front. She tried a different angle.

"I know what you were both talking about that day."

"Immaterial what you think you know. Resorting to blackmail now?"

"Not blackmail." Luutkesr calmly said, wearing a triumphant smile. "A loophole. You have to confirm if what I say is true"

"...are you daft?"

"What?"

"That's not a rule, nor a loophole."

"It isn't?"

"*No.*"

"Ah." Luutkesr tried to think of a rejoinder to that. She couldn't. Against all odds, she had been defeated by an old lady.

"If you have anything else to say to me, I will be at the Shack, *where I should be.*"

One suspect eliminated, two more to go.
