
Luutkesr awoke from bed, tired and grumpy as she usually was during the change of the Cycle. Her coffee dependency kicked in, kicking her out of bed and into some proper clothes. She would have to tackle her case *after* she had had something to eat and drink.

It could definitely wait.

Clothed and ready, she walked from home--Pemdr was *still* asleep--and took almost no time reaching her preferred coffeehouse. No, it was not *The Pinvixr Brothers*, she'd be caught dead before she started taking her coffee there.

Instead, the amateur Investigator entered through the door of *Creamy Cups*, waving to the on-duty barrister in greeting. Unlike the open café seating that favored outdoor seating that *The Pinvixr Brothers* had, this coffeehouse was an enclosed building the size of about two shacks combined together.

The exterior of the coffeehouse was nothing special. Just the usual cubic building with a painted on name and symbol of a teacup overflowing with creamy milk. The interior, on the other hand?

The contrast between the two could not have been anymore apparent.

Most buildings only had about an average of a window per room. Enough to let light in, but few so that you are actually still in the shade. Having a sun that did not budge an inch for most of the year meant it was going to get *hot.*

*Creamy Cups* had many more windows, letting in as much sunlight as possible while still maintaining the broad definition of a building. The furniture was different, too. No straight-backed chairs or level table legs to be found here, all there were in the coffeehouse were approximations of furniture that looked to be plants and trees grown to that shape--having never felt the touch of a carpenter's blade.

Speaking of plants, greenery littered the place, but in all the right ways. Potted plants atop more tables than not. A wreath of flowers hanging over every door. A conveniently placed thick vine where the counter should be. All of these qualities together encapsulated one concept for the coffee house; *Nature.*

And who better to be managing this place than the barrister himself? For he was a walking plant in the shape of a man, compacted vines shaping themselves to muscles. His face was covered by an oak mask, a crude smiling face drawn in chalk across it's otherwise featureless expanse. Of course he would prefer to go without the mask, were it up to him. Unfortunately, he did not look exactly...human.

Maybe that was why not many people came here for their coffee.

He greeted Luutkesr in an extraordinarily cheery voice. "Welcome back, my favorite customer!"

The Human's brow raised. "Don't you mean your *only* customer, Prohubdis?" 

"Now, now, no need to be snippy *before* you've started your day." While his face was hidden, Luutkesr knew without a doubt that Prohubdis was grinning behind that wooden mask. He continued speaking. "Will it be the usual, then?"

Luutkesr took one good look at the menu the man had posted on the wall next to the counter. It only had one item listed.

*Coffee, creamed.*

"Yes."

"Good choice!" He said quite jovially as he began brewing the coffee. Luutkesr watched in fascination as he poured the beans into a basin attached to a contraption. It had glowing green script on it that hurt the eye if one looked at it for too long.

As the coffee was being pressed, seemingly on it's own, Prohubdis leaned on his counter. If his masked face did not show any emotion, his body language showed enough interest to compensate.

"Did you hear about the news? Poor woman."

"Such a shame." Luutkesr agreed. "Kenvr was apparently a well respected woman in the community."

"That's not--Never mind."

Luutkesr glanced around at the notably empty seats. "Still not many people?"

Prohubdis shook his head. "Still. Doesn't help that I'm the only worker here."

"What happened to Hiptiya? Wasn't she going to help?"

"Ms. Prickly Thorns says she isn't interested in the family business. And I can't say I blame her."

The woman sighed. "She couldn't get a mask like--?"

"No." Prohubdis said firmly. "It's not that she can't. It's that she won't. Another thing I can understand. It's not good when someone can't even meet your eyes."

"At least you have me to talk to." Luutkesr lamely said.

"Of course."

*But you would never ask me to take off that mask.* Luutkesr could almost hear Prohubdis saying that in the silence. She switched topics.

"Did you or Hiptiya hear anything of how the death might have happened?"

"I know nothing about it sadly. My sister equally little, I would guess, isolated as she is in that forest. Wouldn't hurt to ask her, though." Prohubdis poured the pressed coffee into a cup.

"Well, you've been a great help." Luutkesr said with some forced cheer as she considered trying to find and talk to Hiptiya before discarding the idea. Prohubdis poured cream into the cup and placed it on the vine-counter.

"Of course I have. That will be two marks."

Half an hour later, a refreshed and much more cheerful Luutkesr walked out of *Creamy Cups*, relieved to have staved off yet another day of coffee withdrawal.

She did forget to get that breakfast, though.

Either way, she was now ready to tackle the day, that dead end notwithstanding. There were many things that needed to be done, not least of all tailing the suspicious Mr. Politician in what was possibly a legally dubious manner. She had *also* promised one of her friends a week back that she would care for his parrot.

Oh yes, the day's challenges were many. She had to find that politician and tail him *discretely*, so that she could implicate the man. Everything matched up about this case! She just had to prove it.

---

First step to proving Mr. Politician's guilt: Actually find the man first.

What was his name? What did he look like? What was it he was politic on? Where did he even live? Luutkesr would assume something like a disgusting swamp or a particularly inviting basket of refuse.

She had certain opinions on the high class.

Of course, Luutkesr doubted that someone like that man would live in a fitting place, but a woman can dream. Instead of coming up with further conjecture on where the politician lived, she went to someone who *did* know where he was.

A visit to the village hall proved quite fruitful as she inquired about 'Mr. Politician What's-His-Name, you know, the one with the limp and the nasty looking nose.'

The receptionist there helpfully directed her to the market street of the village, known as *Golden Street*, but not without a word of warning.

"Do be careful, Bicupsr--" That was his name, "--is in the middle of an event over there. It seems like he's changed his stance on...something. I'm not that up to date with him. He's been awfully busy of late along with every other politician not concerned about the accident." The accident was what they called Kevasr's death.

Luutkesr's face screwed in confusion. "What is happening for everyone to go crazy now?"

Now the receptionist looked at the woman like she was crazy. "Haven't you heard? The election's near."

"The *what*? Didn't we just have one?"

"This one's different. I'm sure you at least heard of Venkgyr's retirement party?"

"..Uh huh."

"I can't believe this. Essentially, she was the old Head Treasurer of the bank here. With her retirement comes a vacuum which everyone seems intent on filling."

"Oh? After the money, then?" Luutkesr said in a conspiratorial tone, already searching for a way to fit this in with her narrative.

"What? No, that doesn't make sense. It's not like you can just scoop money from the vaults willy-nilly. No, it's the *prestige* that comes with that."

Comprehension dawned on Luutkesr's face. "So that shows the monumental amount of trust the people have in the Head Treasurer, and with that comes the love and support."

The receptionist nodded. "Exactly. Which is why I would advise you don't interrupt whatever Bicupsr is doing. He would not take kindly to that, if you catch my meaning."

Luutkesr nodded grimly. While most people would not know it, Luutkesr's people were a nasty bunch when it came to politics.

So the implied threat, while of course inconvenient, was not terribly out of character for any of the politicians here. No, the real surprise was that Bicupsr was even in *Golden Street* to begin with. At least according to the receptionist who was rapidly becoming her new best friend.

"I don't even know why he's over there," She said. "He was a staunch supporter of Grengyr, who advocated for decentralized banking..."

After the first minute, Luutkesr's eyes glazed over as the receptionist pontificated on the intricate politics of this village. When it seemed like she was done talking Luutkesr thanked her and left the village hall.

Step one, done! She knew who her target was, where he was and what he was doing. She also just got an extra motive for the murder.

Bicupsr cuts off the money from Kevin--that was her name, wasn't it?--because she had been spending too much, and he could not afford it if people found out! But how then wouldhe siphoning funds from the bank?

She would ask him herself when the time came.

...after she popped by her friends house for that favor.
